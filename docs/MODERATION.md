# By the Whey Moderation Process

## User controls

- Every tasting and account can be reported from its feed menu.
- Comments and catalog entries have their own report controls.
- New tasting photos are hidden from other users until an administrator approves them.
- Server-side text checks reject clearly harmful, explicit, or spam-like content before publication.
- Blocking immediately removes the blocked account's activity and prevents interaction in both directions.
- Users can manage and reverse their own blocks from Safety & Legal.
- Users can delete their own tastings, comments, photos, and entire account.

## Report review

Administrators review open reports in **Profile → Manage the community → Reports**. Each report includes its type, target identifier, reporter, reason, and date.

Administrators review new photos in **Profile → Manage the community → Photos**. Approval makes a photo eligible for public feed display. Rejection deletes the stored photo and its database record while preserving the tasting.

An administrator must inspect the referenced content and choose:

- **Dismissed:** no violation or insufficient evidence.
- **Actioned:** content was removed, corrected, or the responsible account was restricted.

The report status and reviewing administrator are stored for accountability.

## Photo and content standards

Remove content that contains harassment, hate, threats, sexual exploitation, scams, illegal sales, graphic violence, private identifying information, copyright violations, or material unrelated to the cheese community.

Catalog corrections should be checked against producer information or another reliable source. Good-faith disagreement about a tasting opinion is not a violation.

## Operational process

1. Review new reports regularly during beta and at least once per business day after launch.
2. Prioritize threats, exploitation, private information, and credible safety risks.
3. Remove clearly violating content and record the report as actioned.
4. Use account restrictions only when proportionate to the behavior.
5. Preserve only the minimum information needed to document a moderation decision.
6. Escalate credible imminent danger to the appropriate qualified or legal channel; the app is not an emergency service.

Before public release, assign a named moderation owner, publish a support address, and define response targets for urgent and ordinary reports.
